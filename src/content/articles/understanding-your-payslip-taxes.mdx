If you've just started your first job in Austria, your payslip (<Term de="Lohnzettel" en="payslip" />) probably shows a smaller number than the salary you agreed to. That gap is mostly income tax and social insurance — and it's normal. This article walks through where that money goes and how you might get some of it back.

## Wage tax: paid automatically

Austria uses a pay-as-you-earn system for employees. Your employer calculates <Term de="Lohnsteuer" en="wage tax" />, a form of income tax, and sends it directly to the tax office every month. You never have to calculate or pay it yourself — it's already deducted before the money reaches your account.

The amount depends on how much you earn. Austria uses progressive tax bands, so the more you earn, the higher the tax rate on the portion above each threshold. Low earners pay little or no wage tax; higher earners pay a larger share.

<div class="callout">
  <p>
    Wage tax is different from social insurance contributions, which fund health
    care, pensions, and unemployment support. Both appear as separate deductions on
    your payslip.
  </p>
</div>

## The annual tax return: getting money back

Once a year, you can file an <Term de="Arbeitnehmerveranlagung" en="employee tax assessment" /> — often just called the tax return — with the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" en="tax office" />). This recalculates your tax based on your actual yearly circumstances, and many employees get a refund.

Common reasons you might get money back:

- You worked only part of the year (a new job, a move, parental leave).
- You had work-related expenses, such as commuting costs or professional training.
- You supported a family member or paid certain insurance premiums.

You can file your tax return online through FinanzOnline, the tax office's official portal, for up to five previous years. There's no downside to filing — the tax office cannot ask you to pay more as a result of an employee assessment, only to pay less or the same.

## What to check on your payslip

Your monthly payslip typically lists:

1. Gross salary (<Term de="Bruttogehalt" en="gross salary" />) — the full agreed amount.
2. Deductions for wage tax and social insurance.
3. Net salary (<Term de="Nettogehalt" en="net salary" />) — what actually lands in your account.

If a number looks wrong, ask your employer's payroll contact (<Term de="Personalverrechnung" en="payroll department" />) before assuming there's a problem — small variations are common due to bonuses, overtime, or annual adjustments.
