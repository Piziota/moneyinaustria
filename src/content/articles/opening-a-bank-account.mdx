You'll need a bank account almost immediately after arriving in Austria. Most employers pay salaries only by bank transfer, and many landlords expect rent paid the same way. Here's what to expect when opening one.

## What you'll usually need

Requirements vary slightly by bank, but most will ask for:

- A valid passport or ID.
- Proof of address in Austria, often your registration certificate (<Term de="Meldezettel" en="registration certificate" />), which you get after registering your address with the local authority.
- Sometimes proof of income or your employment contract.

Some banks let you start the process online before you have a permanent address, then finish in person once you're registered.

## Current account vs. savings account

A <Term de="Girokonto" en="current account" /> is your everyday account: salary in, rent and bills out, debit card attached. A <Term de="Sparkonto" en="savings account" /> is meant for money you're setting aside; it may pay a small amount of interest but usually isn't linked to a card for daily spending.

Most newcomers only need a current account to start. A savings account can wait until you have a sense of your monthly budget.

## Understanding account fees

Austrian banks often charge an account maintenance fee (<Term de="Kontoführungsgebühr" en="account maintenance fee" />), billed monthly or quarterly. It's worth comparing a few banks, since fees and included services (like a free debit card, or free transfers) vary. Some banks waive the fee if you're a student or if your salary is paid into the account regularly.

## Direct debits and transfers

Recurring payments such as rent, insurance, and phone contracts are usually set up as a <Term de="Dauerauftrag" en="standing order" /> (a fixed recurring transfer you set up yourself) or a <Term de="Einzugsermächtigung" en="direct debit mandate" /> (permission for a company to withdraw a variable amount, like a utility bill). Both are normal and widely used, so you don't need to pay bills manually every month once they're set up.
